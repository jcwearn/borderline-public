Borderline

What this game records, and what it does not.

Borderline is a daily puzzle run as a personal project. There is no account, no advertising and nothing sold to anybody. It does keep track of how the game is played, because that is the only way to tell a puzzle that is hard from one that is broken.

What is recorded

  • That a round started, and whether it was today's puzzle or a free one
  • The shape of the hole: its two countries, its par, and how many countries the day shut, put in the rough or asked you to reach — so a puzzle that plays badly can be told from one that is merely hard
  • Each guess: whether it was placed, bought, a miss, or turned away for costing nothing — and how far into the round
  • How a round ended — finished with its score, or left unfinished
  • Sharing a result, and building a free round
  • Following the link that asks for a coffee, or waving it away — and how many rounds you had finished when you did
  • Whether the device is touch or pointer, so the phone layout can be judged
  • When something failed: the puzzle not loading, the globe not drawing
  • That the page was opened, and when it was closed — so that arriving and never playing is distinguishable from not arriving at all
  • A random identifier kept in your browser's local storage, so a round tomorrow can be recognised as the same player's as today's — that is what a streak is. Your number of finished rounds and your current and longest streaks are kept against it
  • Ordinary request information: browser, device type, the page that linked you here, and an approximate location worked out from your IP address

What is not

No name, no email address, no account — none is asked for and none exists. No cookies are set. Your IP address is used to work out a rough location and is then discarded rather than stored. Nothing here follows you to any other site, and no data is sold or shared for advertising.

Once you have finished twenty rounds, the results card asks whether you would like to buy the author a coffee. It is an ordinary link to Buy Me a Coffee and nothing of theirs is loaded into this page, so they learn nothing about you unless you follow it — and what happens after you do is between you and them. Wave the ask away with the × beside it and it does not come back.

If you are in Europe, none of that happens

Players in the EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland are not measured at all. No analytics is loaded, no identifier is written to the device, no events are recorded — the decision is made before anything is stored rather than after. What remains is what any web server sees in the course of answering a request, which our host counts by country and which names nobody.

Who processes it

Analytics is PostHog, on their US cloud, acting on our instructions. The site itself is hosted by Cloudflare, whose analytics is where that country-level count lives. Events reach PostHog through this domain rather than through PostHog's own addresses, which is a matter of not letting a blocklist decide which players get counted — the data goes to the same place either way.

Why, and on what basis

The legitimate interest of understanding how the game is played, in order to make it better: which puzzles nobody finishes, where people give up, whether the phone layout works. The information above is what those questions need and no more.

How long

Up to a year, which is as long as the analytics account retains anything.

Turning it off

Turn on Do Not Track in your browser and nothing is recorded at all — the game reads it and stays quiet. Clearing this site's data in your browser throws away the identifier, and you become someone new. Any blocker that stops requests to /ingest also works; the game itself does not need it.

Your rights, and their limits

You can ask what is held about you, ask for it to be deleted, or object to it being collected at all. Be aware of the honest limit: with no account and only a random identifier to go on, there is usually no way to connect what is stored to a person who asks. Clearing the site's data is the fastest way to sever the link yourself.

Questions, or a request: privacy@borderline.golf. If you are in the EU or UK and unhappy with the answer, you may complain to your data protection authority.
